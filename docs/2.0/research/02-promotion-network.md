## 0. Ground rules, confidence key, and a fairness warning

This document is deliberately structured to **separate four different things that critics routinely blur together**:

| Category | Definition | Example in this dossier |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Documented financial exposure** | A verifiable, disclosed position — SEC filing, on-chain, or self-disclosed with corroboration | Cypherpunk's 323,394 ZEC; Naval's 2015 ECC investment |
| **(b) Paid / partnered promotion** | Money changed hands specifically for advocacy, media, or advisory work | ZCG grants; the CoinDesk report sponsored by GenZcash; Cypherpunk's paid strategic advisors |
| **(c) Organic advocacy** | Public opinion with no documented payment or position | Vitalik Buterin's protocol comments; Raoul Pal's remarks |
| **(d) Unverified allegation** | Claimed but not evidenced | "Bot networks"; "coordinated pump" |

**Confidence key:** `HIGH` = primary document or multiple independent sources agree. `MED` = single credible secondary source, or primary source read through an intermediary. `LOW` = single weak source, inference, or contested.

**Fairness warning that should appear on the published site:** Nothing in this dossier evidences illegality. Holding an asset and publicly saying you like it is legal and normal. Being paid a grant to make educational content is legal and, when disclosed, legitimate. The forensically interesting facts here are (1) the **density of overlapping financial relationships** among a small group of promoters, (2) the **absence of position disclosure** in most public advocacy, and (3) the **gap between marketing claims and on-chain reality**. Those are editorial arguments, not accusations of fraud.

Any ZECReveal claim of "mNAV premium extraction" would be **unsupported** on current data; the fairer criticism is **dilution into a discount**. `MED` on the arithmetic (basic vs. FD ambiguity), `HIGH` on the inputs.

### 1.4 The Winklevoss related-party web

This is the most forensically significant structure in the dossier. All of the following are documented:

- **Winklevoss Capital** led the **$58.888M** private placement that created Cypherpunk (Nov 2025). `HIGH`
- **Will McEvoy**, Cypherpunk's CIO and a board member, is a **Principal at Winklevoss Capital**. `HIGH`
- **Winklevoss Capital was the counterparty** in the **$33.33M equity-based** mining-fleet acquisition (18 Aug 2026) — i.e. the lead investor sold assets to the company it seeded, taking **equity** as payment. Neither The Block nor Cointelegraph flagged this as a related-party transaction. `HIGH` on the facts; `MED` that it is legally a related-party transaction requiring Item 404 disclosure (I did not read the underlying 8-K text directly).
- **Winklevoss Capital is an investor in ZODL**; **Cypherpunk also invested $5.0M** in ZODL. `HIGH`
- The **Winklevoss twins personally donated 3,221 ZEC (~$1.2M)** to **Shielded Labs** (21 Jan 2026), their second donation (first in 2023). Shielded Labs is where **Zooko Wilcox** is Chief Product Officer — and Zooko is simultaneously a **paid strategic advisor to Cypherpunk**. [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/people-culture/zcash-developer-shielded-labs-gets-donation-from-winklevoss-twins/) `HIGH`
- **Gemini**, the Winklevoss exchange, lists ZEC, was the **first regulated exchange to support shielded ZEC withdrawals**, and launched a **Gemini Credit Card: Zcash Edition** (29 Jan 2026) paying up to 4% cashback in ZEC. Gemini's ZEC volume rose **45%** post-launch. [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/zcash-surges-gemini-zec-credit-card/) `HIGH`
- The Winklevoss/Zcash relationship **predates the 2025 rally by five years**: a joint Gemini blog interview with Cameron, Tyler and Zooko dates to **7 Oct 2020**. [Gemini](https://www.gemini.com/blog/a-conversation-on-zcash-with-cameron-and-tyler-winklevoss-and-zooko-wilcox) `HIGH` — **this is important for fairness**: their advocacy is long-standing, not invented in 2025.

**The circular structure, stated neutrally:** Winklevoss Capital funds the treasury company → the treasury company buys the token and funds the developer → the developer's founder advises the treasury company → the token's co-founder advises the treasury company and runs a second developer that Winklevoss also funds → the Winklevoss exchange lists the token, adds a rewards card for it, and its founders publicly advocate for it. Every leg is disclosed somewhere; **no single disclosure shows the whole loop.**
